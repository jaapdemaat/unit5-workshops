Aim

Unit 5 runs parallel with Unit 6 and is delivered principally by Contexts tutors. Its purpose is fourfold:

  • Expand and consolidate the forms of writing introduced in Units 2 and 3 — descriptive, reflective, and critical
  • Develop a theoretical and contextual framework for the body of work students are producing in their Unit 6 Platforms
  • Investigate current practice in relation to ethical, cultural, critical and intellectual perspectives on both historical and contemporary Graphic Communication Design
  • Investigate how current practice engages with principles of climate, racial and social justice, and consider the responsibilities of graphic communication designers in producing ethical and sustainable work

Through a sequence of writing tasks and contextual lectures, students use writing to discover their position within the evolving discipline. This frames their creative trajectory and enables informed decisions about subsequent GCD Practice and Platform choices.

The unit culminates in a written and designed critical report.

Learning Outcomes

  • 🎯 LO1 Review, investigate and reflect on the development of your current practice with reference to the body of work you are producing in your Unit 6 Platforms (AC Enquiry)
  • 🎯 LO2 Explore and evaluate a range of historical and contemporary contexts of Graphic Communication Design and investigate how your current practice engages with climate, racial and social justice (AC Knowledge)
  • 🎯 LO3 Produce a designed critical report that uses descriptive, reflective, and critical writing to contextualise your current practice with reference to debates in the larger context of Graphic Communication Design (AC Realisation)

The Three Phases

Unit 5 unfolds across three distinct phases, each with different pedagogical aims. The grouping strategy supports this arc: students are grouped by Unit 6 Project 1 for Phase 1, then transition to new projects while remaining in their Unit 5 group for deeper reflective and critical work.

🗺️ Phase 1: Research Methods (Weeks 1–5)

Students grouped by Unit 6 Project 1. Each week introduces a research method applied directly to their project. By week 5, they have explored research skills and critical vocabulary.

📍 Phase 2: Positioning (Weeks 6–8)

Week 7 brings a shift. Students move to Unit 6 Platform Project 2 and new Platform groups, but stay in their Unit 5 group. Now they examine their own emerging practice instead of focussing only on their Platform project.

📢 Phase 3: Critical Practice (Weeks 9–13)

Students situate their practice critically within broader design conversations and develop their designed critical report.

How Sessions Work

Session format

Two facilitators, two groups of 15+ students per classroom (30+ total). Most sessions begin and end with shared whole-group work (both tutors, all students together). The middle section splits into parallel group work, with each tutor working separately with their 15+ group.

Co-teaching model

Shared moments (both tutors, all students)

  • Opening: introduction and context setting
  • Pre-task discussion & visual references: review pre-task reading, share and discuss visual references related to the week's theme
  • Closing: recap and next week preview

Parallel moments (each tutor with 15+ group)

  • Main workshop activity: mapping, questioning, curating, etc.
  • Tutor circulates, asks/answers questions
Pre-session alignment

Meet 15 min before every workshop:

  • Agree on framing, introduction, and context setting
  • Assign roles: who leads intro, pre-task discussion & visual references
  • Discuss any student concerns and identify additional support needed
  • Logistics: room set-up, print-outs and material needed for the workshop

Room will be set up by GSAs (Graduate Studio Assistants) ahead of the session. This is arranged by David Frame (d.j.frame@csm.arts.ac.uk). Contact David directly for any room issues or requests for a different layout.

All print-out PDFs should be downloaded from the Unit 5 SharePoint folder.

Workshop materials (pens, papers, Post-it notes etc) are available in the F studio offices. If stock is low, please report to Rachel David (r.david@arts.ac.uk).

Before Each Session

Unit leaders' responsibility

Unit leaders send weekly reminders: pre-task details, any special setup, key pedagogical notes.

Facilitators' preparation

  • Read the week's facilitator notes
  • Read and engage with students' pre-tasks
  • Meet co-tutor 15 min before to align

During Each Session

Session timing and arrival

Sessions always begin exactly at 10:00. It sets the professional standard you want students to adopt in their own practice. Ask them to arrive by 9:45 to settle. Start at 10:00 regardless. Close the door to signal the session has started. Late arrivals will catch up.

Attendance

At the start of each workshop: remind students to record their attendance on Seats.

After Each Session

Student follow-up

Reach out within 24 hours via Teams. For example: "I've noticed you didn't make it to class today. Are you ok?" Recommend signing up for support tutorials (listed below).

Workshop summary for Teams

One facilitator (per classroom) posts a summary by end of day (rotate weekly). Keep it warm and specific. Include the workshop summary, why it mattered, and next week's preview.

Communication: Teams

Channels

  • "Unit 5 Dream Team" (staff only) — team updates, decisions, reminders
  • Stage 2 main group (all staff + students) — stage-wide announcements
  • Classroom group (15+ students + 2 tutors) — group updates and workshop summaries

Who posts what

  • Stage & Unit leaders — weekly prep reminders, assessment deadlines
  • Facilitators (alternating) — workshop summary by end of day
  • Pre-tasks — posted by Unit leaders in advance

Getting Immediate Help

First Aid

  • Physical First Aid: go to or call the main reception/front desk of your specific UAL building. Have the location and incident details ready to report. They will contact a trained on-site first aider.
  • Defibrillators: automated external defibrillators (AEDs) are located at main receptions.
  • Life-threatening emergencies: dial 999 immediately. From an internal UAL desk phone, dial 5-999.

Mental Health First Aid

  • Support line: contact the UAL Counselling and Health Advice Service at 020 7514 6426 (Monday–Friday, term-time, 9:30 AM – 4:30 PM)
  • Email: studenthealth@arts.ac.uk
  • On-site help: Mental Health First Aiders are available across all UAL colleges to assist individuals in distress or crisis
